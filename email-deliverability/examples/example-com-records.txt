# Worked Example: example.com

Domain `example.com` sends through Google Workspace for mailboxes and SendGrid for transactional mail. Reports go to `dmarc@example.com`.

## SPF record

Publish as a TXT record at the root of `example.com`:

```
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net ~all
```

Lookup count: Google Workspace 1, SendGrid 2. Total 3 of 10.

## DMARC record

Publish as a TXT record at `_dmarc.example.com`, starting at `none`:

```
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com;
```

## Rollout

1. Publish both records and verify them with a live lookup.
2. Read the aggregate reports at `dmarc@example.com` for at least two weeks. Every legitimate sender must pass.
3. Move `p=none` to `p=quarantine`, watch the reports for two more weeks.
4. Move `p=quarantine` to `p=reject`, and keep the `rua` address in place.

## DKIM

- Google Workspace: set up DKIM in the Workspace admin console. The default selector is `google`.
- SendGrid: authenticate the domain in SendGrid. SendGrid gives the selectors to publish; DKIM then carries DMARC for SendGrid mail, because SendGrid's default return-path does not align for SPF.

## What to check after publishing

- The SPF record returns exactly one record and the lookup total is 3.
- The DMARC record is found at `_dmarc.example.com` with `p=none` and the right `rua` address.
- A DKIM key record exists for each provider's selector.
- After the TTL has passed, re-check with live lookups. DNS changes take time to spread.
